Solid Gold vs Steel Piercing Jewelry: What Lincoln Locals Should Know

Solid Gold vs Steel Piercing Jewelry: What Lincoln Locals Should Know

One of the most common things people say at our counter at Gateway Mall: "Wait, you have gold? Actual gold?" Yes — Edison stocks solid gold piercing jewelry, and it's still rare among Nebraska shops. Here's the honest breakdown of when that matters and when it doesn't.

The vocabulary, decoded

"Surgical steel" sounds medical but is a marketing term as much as a standard. Some surgical steels contain nickel — and nickel is the most common metal allergen on the planet. If your ears turned green and itchy from a mall kiosk earring at 12, that was probably nickel.

"Implant-grade titanium" is the actual medical standard — the stuff used in surgical implants. It's nickel-free (or effectively so), lightweight, and safe for fresh piercings. For a brand-new piercing, implant-grade titanium is the correct default and what you should insist on.

"Solid gold" means actual gold alloy throughout — not plated, not filled. For piercings, the relevant grades are 14k and 18k nickel-safe alloys. Gold is what you choose when the piercing is healed and you want it to look like jewelry, because it does.

When you should pay for gold

  • Your body hates steel. Recurring irritation, bumps, or itching with metal jewelry usually improves dramatically the day you switch to implant-grade titanium or nickel-safe gold.
  • It's a piercing you'll wear forever — nostril, septum, daith, first lobe. A gold nostril screw becomes a piece of you.
  • You wear it to work. Gold reads as "earring," not "hardware." Ask us how many retail managers have thanked us.
  • Gifts. A gold piece in a new piercing is a genuinely great gift, and we wrap it.

When titanium is the smarter buy

  • Fresh piercings, always. Gold can wait until you're healed; titanium is the healing champion.
  • Budget builds. A titanium industrial costs a fraction of a gold one and looks great.
  • Heavily textured or blackline finishes — titanium coatings offer looks gold doesn't.

What it costs, roughly

Implant-grade titanium basics in Lincoln typically run in the same range as quality earrings anywhere. Solid gold pieces run more — think fine-jewelry pricing rather than mall-kiosk pricing — and the exact number depends on the gauge, the stone, and the design. We price transparently at the counter; ask us to show you both options before you commit.

The one warning that matters

Do not buy "gold" jewelry off fast-fashion sites and put it in a piercing — plated jewelry wears through to base metal inside a healing channel, and that's how people end up with an angry ear and a story. If it's going in your body, buy it from a piercer who can tell you exactly what alloy it is.
