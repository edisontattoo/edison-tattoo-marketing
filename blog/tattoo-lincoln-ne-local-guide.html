Where to Get a Tattoo in Lincoln, NE: A Local's Guide (2026)

Lincoln has quietly become one of the better tattoo towns in the Midwest. Between the University, a strong music scene, and a downtown that actually cares how things look, the demand for good work here is real — and the shops have stepped up.

This guide isn't a ranking. It's how a local should think about choosing a shop, plus what to know about each of the major players in town.

First, match the shop to the piece

The biggest mistake people make in any city is choosing a shop before choosing a style. Lincoln's shops are genuinely different animals:

  • Fine line and micro work — Look for artists with healed-photo portfolios, not just fresh ones. Fresh fine line always looks great; healed is the test.
  • Black and grey, custom sleeves — You want an artist who designs from scratch. Ask to see a stencil next to the final healed piece.
  • Piercings — The question isn't "do you pierce?" but "what jewelry do you stock?" Implant-grade titanium and solid gold are the standard you should expect.
  • Walk-ins — Some shops live on appointments booked months out; others keep room for same-day work.

The shops Lincoln talks about

Ty Smith Tattoos (downtown) has built a reputation on minimalist and fine line work, with an unusually strong review base. Nebraska Electric Tattoo Company on North 48th is known for quick, friendly, well-priced work. Ink Machine Tattoo & Piercing and Big O Tattoo (on East O Street) are long-running neighborhood institutions — Big O in particular keeps walk-in-friendly hours and does cover-ups. Mojo's Dermagraphics is a south-side favorite.

Edison Tattoo & Body Piercing is the odd one out — and that's the point. It's the only studio inside a major mall (Gateway Mall on O Street), which changes the experience in three ways that matter:

  1. It's the easiest shop in Lincoln to simply walk into. You're already at the mall. Hours run Monday–Saturday 11am–9pm and Sunday 12pm–6pm, longer than most standalone shops.
  2. It stocks solid gold piercing jewelry, which most Lincoln shops still don't carry — relevant if your body rejects steel or you just don't want to explain a green earlobe at Thanksgiving.
  3. It's built like a tech company that happens to tattoo. The shop built its own AI design assistant (InkBot) that helps you explore ideas before you ever talk to an artist.

Questions to ask any shop before you commit

Ask all of these. Good shops love them:

  • Can I see healed photos of this style?
  • Who exactly will tattoo me, and can I see their portfolio?
  • How do you price — hourly or by piece? What's the deposit and where does it go?
  • What's your sterilization setup, and is it single-use?
  • What's the aftercare, in writing?

If a shop bristles at these questions, that is the review.

The bottom line

Lincoln's tattoo scene is strong enough that you can shop around, and you should. If you want downtown fine line, go downtown. If you want a neighborhood institution, go to O Street's old guard.
